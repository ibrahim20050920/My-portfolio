Case file 01

Meridian Gear Co.

Multi-currency bookkeeping, inventory and reporting in QuickBooks Online

Meridian Gear Co. is an e-commerce reseller selling phone accessories and gadgets on Amazon US, eBay UK and Amazon UAE. The books were kept in PKR, but the money actually landed in USD, GBP and AED — so nothing was usable until it converted correctly. Three years of transaction history, 1,943 lines, came in duplicated, miscategorized, missing dates, with currencies tagged to the wrong line. The job was to clean it, reconcile it, and turn it into reports the owner could read without a translator.

The problem

  • Three years of unreconciled, miscategorized transactions with no audit trail
  • No multi-currency structure — USD, GBP and AED payouts weren't converting into PKR correctly
  • No confirmation that opening balances tied to the prior year's closing balances
  • No FY2026 budget and no department-level payroll visibility
  • No reports an owner, bank or investor could actually use

Case file 02

Everest Apparel Co.

Full engagement: setup, cleanup, 13-month backlog, and reporting

Everest Apparel runs two retail stores — one in Austin, one in Denver — plus wholesale accounts with 15 boutique buyers and about 22 people on payroll. Two problems arrived at once: twelve transactions already entered wrong by an earlier bookkeeper, and thirteen months of bank activity, 686 transactions, that had never been entered into QuickBooks at all. Below is the engagement broken into the ten steps actually worked through, each with the screen that proves it.

01Setting Up the Company›

Created the company under Everest Apparel Co., set industry, fiscal year and currency, then connected accountant access for Reclassify Transactions — needed for the next step.

02Building the Chart of Accounts›

Built retail sales split by store, wholesale sales, cost of goods sold, payroll, rent, utilities, shipping, advertising, and a holding account for anything needing client confirmation — before touching a single transaction.

03Diagnosing What Was Already Wrong›

All twelve existing entries were miscategorized — a $15,600 payroll payment sitting under Legal and Professional Fees, Austin's rent booked to Meals and Entertainment, and personal purchases buried inside Cost of Goods Sold.

04Fixing Them With Reclassify Transactions›

Used Reclassify Transactions to correct all twelve by account in batches — same result, a fraction of the clicks, and a cleaner audit trail than twelve separate manual edits.

05Importing 13 Months of Backlog›

Brought in 686 transactions as four quarterly CSV batches rather than one dump, catching import errors a batch at a time instead of hunting through 686 rows after the fact.

06Automating Categorization With Bank Rules›

Built twenty active bank rules covering payroll, rent, suppliers, ad spend and recurring personal charges — routed to Dividend Disbursed rather than Owner's Draw, since Everest is a corporation.

07Categorizing What the Rules Missed›

Hand-checked what the rules didn't catch: a duplicated Austin rent charge was excluded rather than deleted, and an unlabeled cash withdrawal and an unknown wire were filed to a holding account for client confirmation.

08Reconciling All 13 Months›

Closed all thirteen months against bank statements, one at a time, from August 2025 through August 2026 — every single month landed at a $0.00 difference.

09Recording Wholesale Invoices›

Created invoices for wholesale clients dated ahead of each payment, then matched each payment through Receive Payment — so receivables actually clear instead of just showing income with no paper trail.

10Class Tracking & Final Reports›

Turned on Class tracking split by Austin, Denver and Wholesale — the client's first location-level view of which of its three revenue streams actually made money.

The numbers

  • $593,869 in annual income, correctly split between retail and wholesale
  • $164,483 net earnings, reported accurately after the twelve original errors were fixed
  • 686 transactions entered, categorized and matched — no backlog left
  • 13 straight months reconciled to $0.00
  • First location-level view of profitability the client had ever had
